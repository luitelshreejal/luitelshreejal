Variant 1

Static Assignment with Hard Coverage

The base model answers one question: which people staff which projects? Time, roles, and hours are aggregated away; each project \(j\) exposes an estimated per-person workload \(w_j\) (hours over its lifetime), so the cost of assigning \(i\) to \(j\) is \(c_{ij} = c_i \, w_j\).

\(x_{ij} \in \{0,1\}\) person i is assigned to project j  (\(i \in E_j\))
\[ \operatorname*{maximize}_{x} \quad \alpha \sum_{j,i} f_{ij} x_{ij} \;+\; \beta \sum_{j,i} p_{ij} x_{ij} \;-\; \gamma \sum_{j,i} c_{ij} x_{ij} \]
s.t.
\[ \sum_{i \in Q_{js} \cap E_j} x_{ij} \;\ge\; n_{js} \quad \forall j,s : \pi_{js}\ge 1 \]
\[ \underline{N}_j \;\le\; \sum_{i \in E_j} x_{ij} \;\le\; \overline{N}_j \quad \forall j \]
\[ \sum_{j:\,i \in E_j} x_{ij} \;\le\; \overline{n}_i \quad \forall i \]
\[ \sum_{i \in E_j} c_{ij} x_{ij} \;\le\; B_j \quad \forall j \]
\[ x_{ij} \in \{0,1\} \]

Reading it row by row:

(1)
\[ \operatorname*{maximize}_{x} \quad \alpha \sum_{j \in J}\sum_{i \in E_j} f_{ij}\, x_{ij} \;+\; \beta \sum_{j \in J}\sum_{i \in E_j} p_{ij}\, x_{ij} \;-\; \gamma \sum_{j \in J}\sum_{i \in E_j} c_{ij}\, x_{ij} \]

Objective. Maximize a weighted blend of skill fit, stated preference, and cost, summed over every person–project pair actually assigned. \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are the sliders on the demo page — turning them changes what the solver is willing to trade off, without touching the model's structure.

(2)
\[ \sum_{i \in Q_{js} \cap E_j} x_{ij} \;\ge\; n_{js} \qquad \forall j \in J,\ s \in S : \pi_{js} \ge 1 \]

Skill coverage. For every skill \(s\) that project \(j\) requires, at least \(n_{js}\) of the people assigned to \(j\) must be qualified in it. This is the constraint that makes the problem set-covering: coverage is a property of the team, counted over the union of who's on it — not any single person. → refined into an attributed version below.

(3)
\[ \underline{N}_j \;\le\; \sum_{i \in E_j} x_{ij} \;\le\; \overline{N}_j \qquad \forall j \in J \]

Team size. Bounds how many people can be on project \(j\) at once, in either direction.

(4)
\[ \sum_{j:\, i \in E_j} x_{ij} \;\le\; \overline{n}_i \qquad \forall i \in I \]

Concurrency. Caps how many projects any one person can be staffed on at the same time.

(5)
\[ \sum_{i \in E_j} c_{ij}\, x_{ij} \;\le\; B_j \qquad \forall j \in J \]

Budget. Enforces each project's budget cap.

§ 3.3 · from the paper

Explicit Coverage Variables (skill-load control)

Constraint (2) lets a single versatile person “count” toward every skill simultaneously, which may be unrealistic: one person cannot in practice own five skill areas on one project. To cap the number of skill requirements a person may cover, introduce

\(y_{ijs} \in \{0,1\}\) person i is designated to cover skill s on project j  (\(i \in Q_{js} \cap E_j\))

and replace (2) with:

(6)
\[ \sum_{i \in Q_{js} \cap E_j} y_{ijs} \;\ge\; n_{js} \qquad \forall j,s : \pi_{js} \ge 1 \]

The headcount requirement now counts only people designated to cover skill \(s\) — not everyone merely qualified for it.

(7)
\[ y_{ijs} \;\le\; x_{ij} \qquad \forall j,s,\ i \in Q_{js} \cap E_j \]

You can only be designated to cover a skill on a project you're actually assigned to — \(y\) links back to \(x\).

(8)
\[ \sum_{s:\, i \in Q_{js}} y_{ijs} \;\le\; \kappa \qquad \forall j,\ i \in E_j \]

No one person may own more than \(\kappa\) skill areas on a single project — the actual cap on skill-load.

where \(\kappa\) is the maximum number of skill areas one person may own on a single project. This pattern — an explicit “who covers what” layer under the assignment layer — recurs whenever coverage must be attributed rather than merely counted.

Remark · When Variant 1 suffices

Use Variant 1 for annual or quarterly portfolio staffing where projects are long-lived relative to the planning cycle, workloads are roughly uniform, and the interesting decision is team composition, not scheduling.

Presented above as a refinement of Variant 1's constraint (2). In the demo it's wired into Variant 3 instead: toggle it on there to replace “count everyone qualified” with an exact solve of who covers what (eqs. 6–8), capped at κ skills per person per project — watch the optimal plan change once a versatile generalist can no longer single-handedly satisfy two coverage rows at once.

Variant 3

Soft Coverage, Project Selection, and Penalties

Variant 1 presumes every project must be staffed and can be. In practice demand exceeds supply: the model should choose which optional projects to run, fully staff the mandatory ones, and degrade gracefully — reporting where coverage falls short rather than returning infeasible.

\(w_j \in \{0,1\}\) project j is selected (staffed at all)
\(u_{js} \ge 0\) shortfall in coverage of skill s on project j
\[ \operatorname*{maximize} \quad \sum_j v_j w_j \;+\; \alpha \sum_{i,j} f_{ij} x_{ij} \;+\; \beta \sum_{i,j} p_{ij} x_{ij} \;-\; \gamma \sum_{i,j} c_{ij} x_{ij} \;-\; \sum_{j,s} \phi_{js}\, u_{js} \]
s.t.
\[ \sum_{i \in Q_{js}\cap E_j} x_{ij} \;\ge\; n_{js}\,w_j \;-\; u_{js} \quad \forall j,s:\pi_{js}\ge1 \]
\[ u_{js} \;\le\; \overline{u}_{js} \quad \forall j,s \]
\[ x_{ij} \;\le\; w_j \quad \forall j, i\in E_j \]
\[ \underline{N}_j w_j \;\le\; \sum_{i\in E_j} x_{ij} \;\le\; \overline{N}_j w_j \quad \forall j \]
\[ w_j = 1 \quad \forall j \in J^{\mathrm{man}} \]
\[ x_{ij}, w_j \in \{0,1\}, \quad u_{js} \ge 0 \]

Reading it row by row:

soft coverage
\[ \sum_{i \in Q_{js}\cap E_j} x_{ij} \;\ge\; n_{js}\,w_j \;-\; u_{js} \qquad \forall j,s:\pi_{js}\ge1 \]

Same shape as (2), but a shortfall term \(u_{js}\) can absorb any gap — converting a hard row into a priced one.

slack cap
\[ u_{js} \;\le\; \overline{u}_{js} \qquad \forall j,s \]

Prevents the absurd solution of “staffing” a selected project entirely with slack — e.g. \(\overline{u}_{js}=n_{js}-1\) forces at least one qualified person on every required skill of a selected project.

membership
\[ x_{ij} \;\le\; w_j \qquad \forall j,\ i\in E_j \]

An unselected project (\(w_j=0\)) absorbs no people and forfeits its value \(v_j\).

team size
\[ \underline{N}_j w_j \;\le\; \sum_{i\in E_j} x_{ij} \;\le\; \overline{N}_j w_j \qquad \forall j \]

Same size bounds as (3), scaled by \(w_j\) so an unselected project needs zero people instead of the usual minimum.

mandatory
\[ w_j = 1 \qquad \forall j \in J^{\mathrm{man}} \]

Fixes selection for projects that must run regardless — concurrency (4) and budget (5) still apply unchanged.

Selection

The model performs portfolio triage and staffing jointly, which beats the common two-phase practice of picking projects first and discovering they can't be staffed.

Tiered penalties

\(\phi_{js}\) should be tiered: very high for mission-critical skills (effectively hard), moderate for nice-to-have depth.

Mandatory ties back to hard coverage

Fixing \(\overline{u}_{js}=0\) for \(j \in J^{\mathrm{man}}\) recovers exact hard coverage for that project.

Diagnosis

At optimum, positive \(u_{js}\) are exactly the hiring/training gaps — a ranked shopping list for recruitment, priced at \(\phi_{js}\) per unit.
